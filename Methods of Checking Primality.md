## <u>Prime</u>

A natural number greater than 1 that has exactly two divisors, 1 and itself, is called a prime number. A non-prime number is known as a composite number. 
Any even number other than 2 is composite because every even number can be divided by 2. The number 1 is composite because it has exactly one divisor (1).

## <u>Trial Division</u>

***Idea:*** If an integer *n*, the integer to be factored, can be divided by each number in turn that is less than or equal to the square root of *n*.

> Q. Why square root of *n* instead of *n -1*?
> A composite number can be expressed by *n = a * b*. Here, only one of the two factors, *a* and *b*, can be bigger than the square root of *n*. Because if both are bigger than $\sqrt{n}$, then the product of *a* and *b* will be greater than *n*, which, of course, is not possible. So, if a number is composite, there has to be a factor less than or equal to $\sqrt{n}$. Hence, we check up to the square root of *n*.
